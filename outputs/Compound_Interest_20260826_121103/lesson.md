# Lesson on Compound Interest

## What is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is a way to calculate how much money you earn on your savings or investments. Unlike simple interest, which only pays you on the original amount you put in, compound interest pays you on both your original amount and any interest you have already earned.

### Why Does It Matter?

Understanding compound interest is important because it helps you grow your money over time. The more time your money has to grow, the more you earn. This is especially useful for savings accounts, investments, or loans.

## How Does Compound Interest Work?

Let’s break it down step by step.

### Example Scenario

Imagine you save ₹1,000 in a bank account that offers a compound interest rate of 5% per year. Here’s how it works:

1. **Initial Amount (Principal)**: This is the money you start with. In our case, it’s ₹1,000.

2. **Interest Rate**: This is the percentage the bank pays you for keeping your money with them. Here, it’s 5%.

3. **Time**: This is how long your money stays in the bank. We will look at 3 years.

### Year-by-Year Breakdown

**Year 1**:
- You start with ₹1,000.
- At the end of the year, you earn 5% interest.
- Interest = 5% of ₹1,000 = ₹50.
- Total amount = ₹1,000 + ₹50 = ₹1,050.

**Year 2**:
- Now, you have ₹1,050.
- This year, your interest is 5% of ₹1,050.
- Interest = 5% of ₹1,050 = ₹52.50.
- Total amount = ₹1,050 + ₹52.50 = ₹1,102.50.

**Year 3**:
- Now, you have ₹1,102.50.
- This year, your interest is 5% of ₹1,102.50.
- Interest = 5% of ₹1,102.50 = ₹55.13.
- Total amount = ₹1,102.50 + ₹55.13 = ₹1,157.63.

### Summary of Amounts

At the end of 3 years:
- Year 1: ₹1,050
- Year 2: ₹1,102.50
- Year 3: ₹1,157.63

You can see how your money grows each year. The interest you earn increases because you are earning interest on both your initial money and the interest from previous years.

## The Formula for Compound Interest

The formula to calculate compound interest is:

\[ A = P (1 + r)^n \]

Where:
- **A** = the total amount after n years (including interest)
- **P** = the principal amount (initial money)
- **r** = the interest rate (in decimal form)
- **n** = the number of years

### Example Using the Formula

Let’s use our example in the formula:

- P = ₹1,000
- r = 5% = 0.05
- n = 3

So, 

\[ A = 1000 (1 + 0.05)^3 \]

Calculating this:

1. \( 1 + 0.05 = 1.05 \)
2. \( 1.05^3 ≈ 1.157625 \)
3. \( A ≈ 1000 * 1.157625 ≈ ₹1,157.63 \)

This matches our earlier calculation!

## Conclusion

Compound interest is a powerful way to grow your money. By understanding how it works, you can make smarter choices about saving and investing. The longer you keep your money saved, the more it can grow through compound interest. 

### Key Points
- Compound interest pays you on both your original amount and the interest you earned.
- The longer you save, the more money you earn.
- You can calculate it using a simple formula.

Now you have a basic understanding of compound interest!
